Our History

Charity No. 208709 · Est. 1962

The beginning

We make a living
by what we get,
but we make a life
by what we give.

— Winston Churchill

In 1962, a small group of tea traders gathered in the City of London with a simple conviction: people who have given their working lives to the UK tea trade should never face hardship alone.

They formed the UK Tea Trade Benevolent Society — a registered charity that offers welfare, companionship and practical support to anyone who has worked in the trade, whether they are still behind the desk or long since retired.

More than sixty years later, the mission has not changed. We are still here, still listening, still helping — quietly.

Key moments
1962

The Society is founded by a small group of City of London tea traders who believe the industry should look after its own.

1968

Charity Commission registration — the Society is formally recognised as Registered Charity No. 208709.

1980s

The welfare programme expands to include regular home visits and telephone check-ins for retired members, many of whom live alone.

1995

The 100 Club is launched — a monthly lottery to provide a reliable stream of welfare funding between the biannual events.

2010s

Cocktail parties become the cornerstone of the social calendar — the Spring and Autumn events now regularly draw over 100 guests from across the trade.

2024

Digital transformation begins — online ticketing, membership management and payments bring the Society's operations into the modern era while preserving its personal, confidential ethos.

Today

Still here, still helping — a small team of volunteer trustees continues the work that started over sixty years ago, supporting members the length and breadth of the UK.

How we help

Well-being check-ins

A friendly voice on the phone, a visit when it matters. We keep in regular contact with members so that nobody is left isolated or forgotten.

Practical advocacy

Navigating benefits, pensions, council support and grant applications can be bewildering. We walk alongside members through the process.

Direct financial relief

When unexpected hardship strikes — an emergency boiler, a funeral, a gap between payments — the Society can step in with confidential, no-strings support.
